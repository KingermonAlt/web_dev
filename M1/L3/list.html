Chess Players

A constellation of brilliant minds from chess history.

  • The fierce tactician — bold, precise, and relentlessly attacking.

    Born in 1963, Kasparov became the youngest world champion in 1985. He held the title until 2000 and is remembered for his deep preparation, dynamic attacks, and historic matches with IBM's Deep Blue.

  • The lightning mind — elegant calculation with effortless speed.

    India's first world champion, Anand won the title in 2007 and successfully defended it in several formats. His universal style and remarkable speed earned him the nickname “The Lightning Kid.”

  • The endgame magician — patient pressure and extraordinary resilience.

    Carlsen became world champion in 2013 and dominated the rankings for over a decade. He is celebrated for turning tiny advantages into wins, especially in seemingly equal endgames.

  • The digital dynamo — fearless instincts and electric creativity.

    A five-time U.S. champion, Nakamura is one of the strongest speed-chess players ever. He helped bring elite chess to a new audience through online competition and streaming.

  • The rising star — imaginative attacks and modern ambition.

    Born in 2003, Firouzja became a grandmaster as a teenager and quickly joined the world's elite. His creative attacking play has made him one of the leading talents of his generation.

  • The romantic pioneer — graceful development and timeless brilliance.

    Morphy was a 19th-century American chess prodigy whose 1858 European tour made him famous. His games remain classic lessons in rapid development, open lines, and purposeful sacrifice.
