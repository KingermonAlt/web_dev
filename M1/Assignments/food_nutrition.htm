Building a Healthy Plate

Why a Balanced Meal Matters

A healthy plate gives our body the energy it needs to study, play, think, and grow. It should include colourful fruits, fresh vegetables, grains, and protein-rich food.

Learn more about healthy eating here: MyPlate Nutrition Guide

Smart Snack Choices

Snacks can be healthy when we choose foods that give energy instead of only sugar. Fruits, nuts, sprouts, and yoghurt are better choices than chips and sugary drinks.

Explore more nutrition facts here: WHO Healthy Diet Guide

Nutrition Tips for Students

Drink Enough Water

Water helps the body stay active, supports digestion, and keeps the brain focused. Students should carry a water bottle and drink water throughout the day.

Eat Colourful Food

Different colours in fruits and vegetables give different nutrients. A colourful plate often means a healthier plate.

Read more about healthy food choices here: Nutrition.gov

Quick Nutrition Note

Try this simple rule: fill half your plate with fruits and vegetables, one quarter with grains, and one quarter with protein.

Meal Planning Page

Plan Ahead

Choose a few simple meals before shopping. Planning helps students save time, reduce waste, and make balanced choices during busy weeks.

A Flexible Formula

Combine a whole grain, a protein, vegetables or fruit, and water. Prepare extra portions so tomorrow’s lunch is ready.

Breakfast Page

Start Strong

Try oats with fruit, yoghurt with nuts, eggs with whole-grain toast, or a bean-and-vegetable wrap for a filling morning meal.

Quick Options

Keep bananas, whole-grain cereal, and unsalted nuts available for mornings when there is little time to cook.

Budget-Friendly Foods Page

Shop Smart

Compare prices, use seasonal produce, choose store brands, and build meals around beans, lentils, eggs, oats, and brown rice.

Reduce Waste

Freeze extra bread and ripe fruit, store food correctly, and use leftover vegetables in soups, omelettes, or pasta.

Food Safety Page

Clean and Separate

Wash hands and produce, keep raw meat separate from ready-to-eat food, and clean preparation surfaces after cooking.

Cook and Chill

Cook food thoroughly, refrigerate leftovers promptly, and follow package storage instructions. When in doubt, ask a trusted adult.

Pet Nutrition Page

Balanced Pet Food

Dogs and cats need species-appropriate, complete food made for their life stage. Fresh water should always be available.

Safe Treats

Treats should be occasional and small. Never share chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, or xylitol-containing foods with pets.

Dog Nutrition Page

Healthy Dog Habits

Measure meals, keep a regular routine, and match portions to your dog’s size, age, activity, and veterinary advice.

Watch for Changes

Sudden weight change, vomiting, diarrhoea, or loss of appetite can signal illness. Contact a veterinarian instead of changing food suddenly.

Cat Nutrition Page

What Cats Need

Cats are obligate carnivores and need food formulated for cats. Offer clean water and follow the feeding guidance on the label.

Healthy Portions

Use measured portions and monitor body condition. Ask a veterinarian about wet food, treats, and nutrition for kittens or older cats.

Small Pets Nutrition Page

Rabbits and Guinea Pigs

Hay is essential for rabbits and guinea pigs. Provide suitable fresh greens, clean water, and vitamin C for guinea pigs as advised.

Hamsters

Offer a measured, species-appropriate mix with small amounts of fresh food. Avoid sticky, sugary, salty, or mouldy foods.

Everyday Pet Care Page

Food and Enrichment

Use safe feeding toys and gentle activity to encourage natural behaviour. Keep food bowls and water containers clean.

Professional Advice

Each pet is different. A veterinarian can recommend portions and diets for allergies, medical conditions, growth, and weight management.

Nutrition Resources Page

Reliable Information

Use guidance from registered dietitians, public-health organisations, and licensed veterinarians rather than unverified social-media claims.

Keep Learning

Read food labels, ask questions, and make gradual, practical improvements. Healthy habits are built through consistency, not perfection.

Vitamins and Minerals Page

Small Nutrients, Big Jobs

Vitamins and minerals help release energy, support bones, protect cells, and keep the immune system working. Eat a variety of vegetables, fruit, beans, whole grains, dairy or fortified alternatives, nuts, seeds, and protein foods.

Food is usually the best source. Supplements are not automatically safer or better, so discuss them with a qualified health professional.

Build Variety

Dark green vegetables can provide folate and vitamin K, citrus and peppers provide vitamin C, and dairy or fortified alternatives can provide calcium. Different foods supply different nutrients, so repeating one “superfood” is unnecessary.

Reading Food Labels Page

Compare Like with Like

Check the serving size first, then compare calories, fibre, protein, saturated fat, sodium, and added sugars. A package may contain more than one serving, even when it looks like a single portion.

Ingredient Lists

Ingredients are listed by weight. Look for whole grains, vegetables, beans, and recognisable foods near the beginning. Use the label as a helpful tool rather than judging one food as “good” or “bad.”

Hydration and Exercise Page

Drink Regularly

Drink water with meals and keep a bottle nearby during school or work. Thirst, a dry mouth, dark urine, headache, and tiredness can be signs that more fluid may be needed.

Active Days

Water is often enough for everyday activity. During prolonged, intense exercise or very hot weather, ask a coach, parent, or health professional about replacing fluids and electrolytes.

A Healthy Food Mindset Page

Balance, Not Perfection

Enjoying favourite foods can fit into a healthy pattern. Aim for regular meals, variety, and enough food rather than strict rules or guilt.

Listen and Ask

Notice hunger and fullness, eat without rushing when possible, and ask a trusted adult or registered dietitian for help with allergies, medical diets, or concerns about eating and body image.
